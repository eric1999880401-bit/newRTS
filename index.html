Fixed-angle filming guide

Use the same setup every time. Consistency matters more than a perfect camera.

Side view

  • Camera 2.5-4 m from the lifter, exactly from the side, not 45 degrees.
  • Lens height around hip to lower chest.
  • Keep the whole body, bar, and plates visible from start to lockout.
  • Best for depth, torso angle, hip/knee timing, tempo, bar path, and velocity curve.

Front view

  • Camera 2.5-4 m in front or behind, centered on the lifter.
  • Both shoulders, hips, knees, and feet must stay in frame.
  • Avoid rack uprights blocking knees or hips.
  • Best for left-right shift, knee cave proxy, shoulder/hip height difference, and stance balance.

Bench press

  • Side view should show bar, wrist, elbow, chest touch, and plates.
  • Keep the camera still and do not zoom during the set.
  • If checking uneven lockout, add a front or foot-end video later.

Calibration

  • Plate diameter mode uses 0.45 m as the default reference.
  • If you know another distance, choose Known distance and enter meters + pixel distance.
  • If unsure, use Relative only. The app will still track trends, but not true m/s.
Quick rule: one lifter in frame, bright room, fixed phone/tripod, 30-60 fps, no mirror angle, no moving camera. If confidence is low, treat AI/RPE output as a hint only.
